Council Verdict — How ArkData should compete with Viktor

5 independent advisors · anonymized peer review · chairman synthesis · 2026-06-14

Question: ArkData (pixel-verified person-level attribution; partner-payouts wedge; mock-data, real-data wiring blocked) vs Viktor ($75M Accel-funded AI coworker running Meta/Google ads from Slack, shallow attribution, ~$15M ARR/12k teams). Options: A conversational Slack agent · B integrate as the measurement layer · C double down on measurement + payouts wedge · D become an action layer. What to pursue, sequence, and avoid?

⚖️ Recommendation

Pursue C as the business, framed through the referee / source-of-truth lens (you adjudicate money that moves on verified conversions — agencies & partner networks, not SMBs). Use B as positioning ("the verified truth action layers are graded against") built as an independent standard, not dependent on Viktor's goodwill. Keep A as a thin verified-alert surface only. Do NOT do D

Gated on two unproven assumptions: a buyer in real pain exists, and the pixel delivers defensible verified truth on real data. So the next work is proof, not features.

▶ The one thing to do first

Land one real design partner — an agency or partner-network that already pays partners and disputes attribution — wire their real Meta data + DB, and show one real "over-attribution → verified-payout" delta in dollars. That single proof validates the buyer, the pixel's credibility, and the wedge at once. Everything else waits behind it.

Where the council agrees

  • Never become an action layer (D) — unwinnable vs Viktor's 140 actions/3,200 integrations, and it destroys neutrality (a referee who plays can't be trusted).
  • The partner-payouts wedge (C) is the business, not a feature — verified conversions where money changes hands (agencies/networks, not SMBs).
  • Reframe the category — you're the referee/scorekeeper, not a faster horse. Distribution is Viktor's game; adjudication is yours.
  • Data is the binding constraint, not strategy — mock data is the real competitor. "Until one tenant sees one real conversion, you're a demo."
  • Fix the pitch — lead with money ("get paid for conversions that are actually real, not the ones Meta inflated"), not jargon.

Where the council clashes

  • Option A: Expansionist wants it shipped fast as a "trust oracle"; Outsider/Contrarian/Executor say no (Viktor's turf, vaporware on mock data). Resolution: A only as a thin verified-number alert, never a conversational clone.
  • Option B: framed as leverage vs. a copyable feature. Peer review broke the tie: Viktor won't voluntarily integrate the layer that grades it 40% fiction — so B works only as an independent standard buyers demand, not "feed Viktor and hope."

Blind spots the peer review caught

  • No customer evidence — does a design partner with a live attribution/payout dispute exist now? Validate before building.
  • Verification credibility is unproven — post-iOS14 / cookie-deprecation / server-side CAPI, is the pixel genuinely more truthful, or a different bias? The whole "right to be believed" rests on this being provable.
  • Viktor's clock — $75M can bolt on or acquire "good-enough verified" before ArkData unblocks data.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian — don't fight on distribution; truth isn't a purchase

Viktor is at $15M ARR in 10 weeks; ArkData has mock data and no real Meta token wired. This isn't a fair fight on distribution — don't pick the fight that requires winning on it. Fatal flaw: the brief treats "measurement truth" as a moat, but nobody buys truth — they buy money. Viktor's shallow attribution wins because it's attached to an action that moves spend TODAY. Reconciliation tells an agency they over-paid last quarter — an accusation, not a purchase. Verified payouts only matter if a partner network already exists and fights about money — have you talked to five such customers? NOT D (suicide). NOT A as primary (Viktor's UX without Viktor's distribution, on mock data = vaporware). B makes you a feature a $75M company copies in a sprint. So C, ruthlessly narrowed: pick ONE partner-network vertical that already pays partners and disputes attribution; wire real data for THEM first. Mock data is your real competitor, not Viktor.

The First Principles Thinker — you're the referee, not a racer ★ strongest

Stop — they're not in the same race. Viktor sells distribution/convenience (commoditizes when Meta ships the buttons natively). ArkData sells "the right to be believed" — an adjudication function. When real money moves (payouts, agency billing, over-attribution disputes), someone must be the source of truth both sides accept. The real question isn't "how do I compete with Viktor" — it's "who needs a referee, and will they pay?" Agencies/partner networks settling money; not SMBs. C is the business (a system of record, not a dashboard). B is the moat dressed as humility — be the truth Viktor's actions are graded against; action layers NEED an independent referee. NOT D — becoming an action layer destroys neutrality. A only as an interface to truth you own. You're not the faster horse — you're the scorekeeper. Don't enter the race.

The Expansionist — verified truth compounds; own the clearing house

Don't frame this as "compete." Viktor spent $75M proving 12,000 teams will wire ad spend into a Slack agent — then handed them attribution so shallow it exports to Sheets. That's a top-of-funnel machine generating buyers about to discover their action layer optimizes against fiction. Verified truth compounds; actions race to zero margin. Lean into B AND C: be the "verified-by-ArkData" substrate every action agent must cite — like Stripe Radar/Plaid underneath everyone. Partner-payouts is a category Viktor structurally can't enter — a wedge into the $14B+ affiliate economy where verified conversions ARE the product. Ship "Ask ArkData" fast as the trust oracle: "Viktor wants to 3x this budget; ArkData says 40% is double-counted" — the second opinion every buyer screenshots. NOT D.

The Outsider — fix the pitch; sell the money sentence

Cold pitches: Viktor — "I'll run your ads from Slack" (four words). ArkData — "pixel-verified person-level deterministic attribution with reconciliation to one truth via the measurement trifecta" (needed a dictionary). That's the real problem. A media buyer wants to spend less and make more today, not "truth." Viktor does a thing; ArkData explains a concept. Do NOT build "Ask ArkData" — Viktor's home turf, a chatbot returning paragraphs; you'd lose and blur your difference. Do NOT chase D. Do C as foundation, B as go-to-market. The payouts wedge is the only thing a normal person instantly values: "you get paid for conversions that are actually real, not the ones Meta inflated." Then B: "Viktor pulls the levers, ArkData tells it which levers are real." But first — rewrite the pitch to one plain sentence a tired ad buyer nods at.

The Executor — data is blocked; here's Monday

Pursue C, with a thin slice of A — only what's buildable Monday without real data. Real-data wiring is BLOCKED, which kills B and D outright (B needs a live verified feed; D needs writing to ad accounts you can't even read yet). What you CAN do Monday: (1) Harden the wedge — payout ledger tied to verified conversions; UI/logic problem, runs fine on mock. (2) Unblock the Meta token + DB — highest leverage; until one tenant sees one real conversion, you're a demo. (3) Upgrade the "Route to Slack" stub into one anomaly alert that fires with a verified number. Do NOT chase integration count, build a conversational agent, or write to ad accounts. Monday: token first, payout ledger second, one real Slack alert third.

Peer-review highlights

Unanimous: First Principles (referee/adjudication) is the strongest answer — only one with a single coherent principle across all four options.

Biggest blind spot (4/5): the Expansionist's "be the substrate everyone cites / ship Ask ArkData fast" ignores that real data is blocked — a substrate vision with no substrate.

What all five missed: zero customer evidence (does the paying referee-buyer exist now?); Viktor's clock (could acquire/bolt on "good-enough verified" first); and whether ArkData's pixel can actually prove person-level truth post-iOS14/cookie-deprecation/CAPI — or is just a different bias. Also: Viktor won't integrate the layer that grades it as fiction.
